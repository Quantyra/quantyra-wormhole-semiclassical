## Physical operator and the joint lattice

[BFR, sections 2.1.1--2.1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.00871v2)
gives `q` unit-charge LLL fermions of spin `j=(q-1)/2` and a boundary
bilinear that transfers them between the two sides. In a covariant
boundary-frame notation it has the form

```
H_int(U) = -h(U) [sum_mn psi_R,m^dagger U_mn psi_L,n + h.c.],
U = exp(i alpha) D^j(g).
```

Constant phases and normalization depend on the spinor convention. This
equation identifies the representation and transfer, not an exact
identification of `h` with the earlier rotation angle `theta`. With an
explicit link, `U -> V_R U V_L^dagger`; this makes the bilinear invariant
under endpoint changes of gauge frame. At the actual AdS boundaries the
charged boundary operators are already invariant under bulk gauge
transformations that vanish there, and the coupling selects relative
global frames. Adding an independent link rotor to those already dressed
operators without a Gauss constraint can double count degrees of freedom.
[Harlow, section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1510.07911v2) gives the relevant
distinction between a link and separately dressed charged operators.

For a neutral spin-zero magnetic seed, unit-charge LLL matter and
integer-spin neutral excitations, the states generated by these fields
obey

```
2 l = (q-1) Q  (mod 2).                                    (1)
```

Indeed a charged LLL insertion changes `Q` by one and carries spin `j`;
the SU(2) fusion rule changes `2l` by `2j` modulo two. Integer-spin
neutral insertions preserve the relation. This is a statement about this
chosen seed and field content. A different seed representation, neutral
fermions or additional charged species can change the allowed sector.
It is not an assumed universal charge lattice of quantum gravity.

For even `q` the faithful compact group for these representations is
`G=(U(1) x SU(2))/Z2`, with kernel generated by `(-1,-1)`; for odd `q`
it is `G=U(1) x SO(3)`. The character of the transfer representation is
`exp(i alpha) chi_j(g)`, which descends to either group. Formula (1)
therefore permits the transfer, including for even `q` when charge and
spin parity cannot be summed independently. A state with each individual
electric charge fixed instead has `P_Q U P_Q=0`. Fixing only the pair's
total charge does not impose that zero.

## Exact compact thermal statement

Consider `L^2(G)` and the explicitly assumed dimensionless Hamiltonian

```
Hcal = beta H_compact = x Q^2 + a L^2,
x = beta/(2 K_e),  a = beta/(2 I),  lambda = x+a j(j+1).
```

Let `rho=exp(-Hcal)/Z`. Multiplication by the `q x q` unitary matrix
`U=exp(i alpha)D^j(g)` has entries `U_AB`. Define the thermal midpoint
transfer correlator

```
C(tau) = (1/q) sum_AB Tr[rho^(1/2) U_AB(tau)
                        rho^(1/2) U_AB^dagger],
U_AB(tau)=exp(i Hcal tau) U_AB exp(-i Hcal tau).
```

At zero time this is real and positive. With the usual conjugate-side
TFD pairing it is the normalized two-sided transfer correlation. Equal
forward evolution of the two sides for physical time `U_phys` gives
`tau=2 U_phys/beta`. Equivalently the relative-link heat-kernel wave
function evolves with the sum of the two compact Hamiltonians.

For every positive `x,a`, every allowed `q>=2`, and either group above,

```
0 <= C(0) <= 1,       1-C(0) <= lambda/4,                   (2)
M_2 <= 2 lambda,      Re C(tau) >= 1-lambda/4-lambda tau^2. (3)
```

Here `M_2` is the second dimensionless frequency moment of the positive
midpoint spectral measure. These bounds do not assume independent
electric and angular heat kernels, small spin, a continuum charge sum
or a classical random orientation.

Proof. In an energy basis let `p_n=exp(-E_n)/Z` and
`W_mn=(1/q) sum_AB |(U_AB)_mn|^2`. Pointwise matrix unitarity gives
both row and column sums of `W` equal to one. Thus

```
C(tau) = sum_mn sqrt(p_m p_n) W_mn exp[i(E_m-E_n)tau],
1-C(0) = (1/2) sum_mn (sqrt(p_m)-sqrt(p_n))^2 W_mn.
```

Set `Delta=E_m-E_n`. The energy-weighted thermal sum is exactly

```
S = sum_mn (p_n-p_m) Delta W_mn = 2 lambda.                (4)
```

To obtain (4), average the forward and reverse conjugations of
`Hcal`. Electric shifts give `x[(Q+1)^2+(Q-1)^2-2Q^2]=2x`.
For the spin Laplacian the two multiplication operators contribute
`2a j(j+1)`; the terms with one derivative cancel upon the matrix-index
sum (`Tr D^dagger L_i D=0`). This is an operator identity for the
sum of forward and reverse maps. It respects the quotient since every
matrix entry maps allowed representations to allowed representations.
Thermal traces of these polynomial differential operators converge.

The scalar inequality
`(sqrt(p)-sqrt(r))^2 <= (p-r)(log(p)-log(r))/4`
follows from `tanh(v)<=v` for `v>=0`. It bounds the second expression
above by `S/8`, proving (2). Also
`(p_n-p_m)Delta=2 sqrt(p_m p_n)Delta sinh(Delta/2)`
is at least `sqrt(p_m p_n)Delta^2`. Hence `M_2<=S`.
Finally `cos(v)>=1-v^2/2` proves (3). No estimate of a thermal
spectral gap is used. The argument is a thermal-affinity/commutator
inequality, not a new general quantum-information principle.

An explicit expression suitable for an independent verification is

```
E_Ql = x Q^2 + a l(l+1),
Z = sum_(Q,l allowed) (2l+1)^2 exp(-E_Ql),
C(tau) = [1/(q Z)] sum_(Q,l,l' allowed)
  (2l+1)(2l'+1) N_(l,l')^j
  exp[-(E_Ql+E_(Q+1,l'))/2]
  exp[i tau (E_(Q+1,l')-E_Ql)].                            (5)
```

`N_(l,l')^j` is one when the triangle and parity fusion conditions hold,
and zero otherwise. The two states in a summand have charges `Q,Q+1`.
Charge conjugation and exchange of `l,l'` make (5) real. At zero time,
the same answer is the group integral of
`|K_(1/2)(g)|^2 chi_transfer(g)/(q Z)`, where the heat kernel sums only
representations allowed by (1). This identity checks both the weights
and the `q` normalization.

## Consequence for the selected family

Assume here, without promoting it to a matching result, that the compact
inertias are the fixed-source static coefficients in
[the angular response calculation](angular-boundary-response.md).
Their lower bounds imply `x<=x_e,bare` and
`a j(j+1)<=chi_J,upper`. At `k=2000`,

```
x_e,bare = 2.08333333329e-5,
chi_J,upper = 6.24999999949e-5,
lambda <= 8.33333333278e-5.
```

For `T=N eta_f z_0/(2pi phi_r)`, `z_0=1/2`, and
`Delta_U=delta phi_r/(N eta_f)`, `delta<=1/2`,
`tau_max=2T Delta_U=delta z_0/pi<=1/(4pi)`. Equations (2)--(3) give

```
1-C(0) < 2.084e-5,
sup_(0<=U_phys<=Delta_U) [1-Re C(2T U_phys)] < 2.14e-5.    (6)
```

The parameter result is uniform for integer `k>=2000`. The flux equation
`X(1+3X)=pi k^6` gives
`x_e,bare<1/(24k)` and `chi_J,upper<1/(8k)` directly: substitute
`d_lower=X+5/6` in the earlier formulas, and compare their positive
quadratic polynomials in `X`. Thus `lambda<1/(6k)` and (6) follows
without summing the enormous physical spin. A larger static electric
or angular inertia only improves this conditional bound. Corrections to
the compact Hamiltonian, sector weights or fermion factorization are
not covered by (6).

## What the matrix junction actually does to stress

For a prescribed classical group history, a nondispersive unitary
extension of the earlier two-endpoint rotation is

```
R = [[cos(theta) 1, -sin(theta) U],
     [sin(theta) U^dagger, cos(theta) 1]].
```

Use the same prepared free incident strip covariance as in
[the switching proof](switching-radiation-capture.md), with each block
multiplied by the `q x q` identity. Before the first returned signal,
point splitting gives the exact total flux per real field

```
F_t/N = [theta_dot^2 + sin(theta)^2 v^2
         - theta_dot c - sin(theta)cos(theta) c_dot]/(4pi), (7)
c = Re Tr(U)/q,     v^2 = Tr(U_dot U_dot^dagger)/q >= 0.
```

The singular same-endpoint covariance supplies
`Tr(R_dot R_dot^dagger)=2q(theta_dot^2+sin(theta)^2 v^2)`.
The smooth opposite-endpoint covariance supplies
`Tr(R_dot J R^dagger)=-2q(theta_dot c+sin(theta)cos(theta)c_dot)`.
These identities prove (7) with the earlier real-field normalization
`N=2N_f q`. A constant link retains the same positive switching term
and multiplies the negative term by `c`. A time-varying link also changes
the derivative term and produces positive orientation-motion stress.

As a restricted tolerance check, replace only the initial cross covariance
by a constant factor `C in [1-epsilon,1]`. The original capture bootstrap
is unchanged and its work identity becomes
`e_s=-C a_s z+4pi eta_f a_s^2`. Consequently

```
e_f <= -3/32 + (11/16) epsilon.                            (8)
```

The initial compact deficit in (6) is much smaller than this restricted
capture tolerance. This checks the significance of the number; it is
not a bound on the complete quantum evolution. In particular one may
not insert the freely evolved `C(t)` from (3) into just the linear term
of (7), discard the other terms, and claim the original JT theorem.

For a quantum link, the interaction changes its conjugate charges and
entangles it with the fermions. Its subsequent joint state is not the
free compact thermal state used above. A prescribed classical history
has no such backreaction. Computing the quantum stress needs the actual
Gauss-constrained state and the renormalized mixed link/fermion
correlators. Nor may the on-shell interaction energy be added again to
the backreacted geometric Hamiltonian; BFR section 3.1 explicitly
corrects that double counting in the earlier construction.

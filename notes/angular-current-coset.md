# Local current coset and unequal exterior returns

2026-10-11, E003/S053 continuation. The electric and rotational currents
leave a large local commuting stress sector in the massless lowest Landau
level (LLL) theory. Its chiral central charge is `q-4+6/(k+2)`, where
`k=q(q^2-1)/6`. This is an application of established current algebra,
not a new mechanism for wormhole support. The physical state does not
follow by replacing the number of free channels by this central charge.

The leading diagonal exterior already fails to preserve the linear
electric/rotational current space. The relevant vertex has two frequencies.
For midpoint point-dipole delays, its normalized longitudinal squared
leakage is asymptotic to `3 pi kappa |omega2-omega1| delta`, whereas the
transverse leakage is `o(delta)`. Equal-frequency conjugation misses the
longitudinal effect entirely. This is a result about a specified free
return vertex, not an interacting stress estimate or a physical damping
theorem. Continue the physical long-return target and its matched-pulse
fallback with this distinction retained.

## Local algebra and what it protects

Take q complex massless Dirac channels on a common local two-dimensional
geometry. Each chirality carries spin `j=(q-1)/2` under sphere rotations.
For q>=2 choose Hermitian matrices J_a with

```text
[J_a,J_b]=i epsilon_abc J_c,
Tr(J_a J_b)=T_q delta_ab,   T_q=q(q^2-1)/12,   k=2T_q.
```

Normalize the chiral fermion contraction to `psi_i(z) psi_j^dagger(0)
~delta_ij/z`, and write `j_X=:psi^dagger X psi:`. Wick contraction gives

```text
j_X(z) j_Y(0) ~ Tr(XY)/z^2+j_[X,Y](0)/z.
j_0=j_I,       j_a=j_(J_a).
j_0(z)j_0(0) ~ q/z^2,
j_a(z)j_b(0) ~ (k/2)delta_ab/z^2+i epsilon_abc j_c(0)/z.
```

The electric and rotational algebras commute. Nested current normal
ordering, including the non-Abelian dual-Coxeter shift, yields

```text
T_U1=:j_0 j_0:/(2q),                  c_U1=1,
T_SU2=sum_a :j_a j_a:/(k+2),          c_SU2=3k/(k+2),
T_C=T_free-T_U1-T_SU2,                c_C=q-4+6/(k+2).       (1)
```

One can verify the coefficient in the second line by commuting its modes
with j_b,n: the central term contributes k and the structure-constant
contraction contributes 2. Thus both Sugawara tensors give their own
currents conformal weight one. T_free gives the same transformation,
so T_C commutes with every electric and rotational current mode.
The two Sugawara tensors commute with each other. Subtracting them from
T_free therefore gives a Virasoro algebra with the central charge in (1).
This is a unitary commutant inside the free-fermion vacuum representation.
For q=2,3 its stress state has zero norm and vanishes. For q=4 the charge
is 1/2, for q=5 it is 14/11, and it is positive for all q>=4. At q=1
there is no rotational current and the electric subtraction leaves zero.

In this local theory a gauge interaction built only from j_0,j_a and
gauge variables commutes with the coset modes. Gauge-only kinetic and
quadratic seagull terms do not change that statement. It does not prove
that the physical Hilbert space is an unrestricted tensor product: allowed
current/coset representations, Gauss constraints, compact sectors and
boundary conditions still have to be imposed. For a compatible common
circle of circumference L in the coset vacuum, the two chiralities alone
would give `E_C=-pi c_C/(6L)`. This conditional cylinder formula is not
the full wormhole stress, anomaly or state selection.

There is a further global distinction even at the matter-representation
level. For even q the kernel of `U(1) x SU(2)` acting on these fermions is
generated by `(exp(i pi),-I)`. For odd q>=3 the SU(2) center alone acts
trivially. This identifies the faithful image on the retained fields;
it does not select the four-dimensional gauge group, bundles, spin
structure or a Wilson-line wave function.

Nor does (1) protect against all nonspherical photons. Under angular
commutation, traceless matrices have spins l=1,...,q-1. A rank-l current
with l>=2 is orthogonal to I,J_a. Its current state is an SU(2) affine
primary of spin l: positive modes annihilate it because the only possible
central contraction is Tr(J_a X)=0. Consequently

```text
h_SU2=l(l+1)/(k+2),     h_U1=0,     h_C=1-l(l+1)/(k+2).     (2)
```

For example `X=J_z^2-j(j+1)I/3` has l=2 and nonzero coset weight when
q>=4. For q>=5 every l>=2 in this matrix decomposition has positive
coset weight; at q=4 the l=3 weight is zero. Thus currents outside the
linear gauge-current span are not themselves a projection onto coset
states, although generically they act on that sector. The higher angular
vertices retained in [the angular-interaction note](angular-current-renormalization.md)
cannot be discarded using (1).

## The exterior acts on a bilinear at two frequencies

Freeze the leading point-dipole geometry and use the corrected reflection
reference of [the gluing note](gluing-support-compatibility.md). Retain only
its free, diagonal, lossless return. Frequencies below are real and measured
in inverse throat-scale units. Put

```text
u_n=(n+1/2)/q,  m_n=n-(q-1)/2,  D_n=delta f(u_n),
delta=d/ell,    R(omega)=diag(exp(i omega D_n)),
M_a(omega1,omega2)=R(omega1)^dagger J_a R(omega2).           (3)
```

A bilinear joining Fourier frequencies omega1 and omega2 has precisely
this matrix. Its frequency transfer is eta=omega2-omega1. There is no
reason to set eta to zero in a dynamical current response. Let P_G be the
Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonal projection onto the complex linear space
`G=span{I,J_x,J_y,J_z}` and define

```text
L_a=||(1-P_G) M_a||_HS^2/||J_a||_HS^2.
```

This measures linear current-space closure, not a fraction of the stress
or a probability of creating a coset excitation. Direct projection gives

```text
alpha_q=sum_n m_n^2 exp(i eta D_n)/T_q,
L_z=1-|alpha_q|^2-|sum_n m_n exp(i eta D_n)|^2/(q T_q).     (4)
```

Choose J_+ with nonzero entry `(n+1,n)` equal to
`sqrt((n+1)(q-n-1))`. Let `a_n=(n+1)(q-n-1)`, so `sum a_n=k`.
Then

```text
L_+=1-|sum_(n=0)^(q-2) a_n
           exp(i[omega2 D_n-omega1 D_(n+1)])/k|^2.          (5)
```

Only the J_+ direction overlaps this subdiagonal; projection onto J_x,J_y
is equivalent to projection onto J_+,J_-. J_- exchanges the two delays.
The squared leakages for J_x and J_y are each `(L_++L_-)/2` because the
two diagonals are orthogonal. At eta=0, L_z=0 exactly. L_+ need not vanish:
the difference between neighboring delays still matters. For common
delays, every leakage vanishes at every frequency pair.

## Uniform bounds for point-dipole delays

The established prolate integral in the gluing note gives
`f(u)=g(u)/u`, `1<=g<=kappa`, `g(0+)=kappa`, with

```text
kappa=1+asinh(sqrt(3))/(2sqrt(3)).
```

Its nonnegative integrand F obeys, with the geometric integration variable
t, `a=1-t^2`, `Q=sqrt(a^2+4u^2 t^2)` and `z=a/Q`,

```text
-u partial_u log F
 =(1-z)[2z+a^2/(a^2+2t^2(a+Q))]
 <=(1-z)(2z+1)<=9/8.
```

Differentiation at u>0 and the same integrable bound used there show
`-u g'(u)<=9g(u)/16`. Therefore, with `c_f=25 kappa/16`,

```text
0<-f'(u)<=c_f/u^2,
|D_(n+1)-D_n|<=c_f delta q/[(n+1/2)(n+3/2)].              (6)
```

For J_+ put x=n+1. Its probability weight satisfies
`a_n/k<=6x/(q^2-1)`. Set

```text
C=2 kappa |eta| delta q,
B=(4/3)c_f max(|omega1|,|omega2|) delta q,
S=6/(q^2-1) sum_(x=1)^(q-1) x min(4,C^2/x^2),
A=6/(q^2-1)[2B+2sqrt(2B)].                                (7)
```

The phase in (5) splits into eta D_n and a neighboring-delay difference.
The first variance is at most S, since `D_n<=2 kappa delta q/x` and
`|exp(it)-1|^2<=min(4,t^2)`. By (6) the second is bounded by the sum
of `6 min(4x,B^2/x^3)/(q^2-1)`. The function in this sum is unimodal;
its integral over the positive line is 2B and its maximum is
`2sqrt(2B)`. A nonnegative unimodal sum is at most integral plus maximum,
so its variance is at most A. For unit-modulus random variables,
`Var(XY)<=2 Var(X)+2 Var(Y)`; expand around `(E X)(E Y)` and use
`|E X|<=1`. No independence is needed. Thus

```text
L_+, L_-, L_x, L_y <= min(1,2(S+A)),
L_+, L_-, L_x, L_y <= min(1,A) when eta=0.                 (8)
```

The sum defining S equals
`6[2N(N+1)+C^2(H_(q-1)-H_N)]/(q^2-1)`, with
`N=min(q-1,floor(C/2))` and H_0=0. Similarly (4), before the additional
identity projection, gives

```text
L_z <= min(1,3q/(q^2-1) sum_(x=1)^q min(4,C^2/x^2)).      (9)
```

For fixed frequencies and the joint limit `delta->0`, `q delta->infinity`,
these imply

```text
L_z=O(|eta| delta),
L_x,L_y=O(delta^2[1+log(1/delta)]+delta/q)=o(delta).        (10)
```

At eta=0 use the sharper bound in (8); all formulae remain valid with
zero C or B. These are bounds for real-frequency unitary matrices (3).
They do not extend automatically to a complex-frequency resolvent.

## Sharp longitudinal tail coefficient

In the same joint limit, at fixed real frequencies,

```text
L_z/delta -> 3 pi kappa |eta|.                            (11)
```

Here is an ordinary proof for the actual point-dipole f, not only its
inverse-tail approximation. First replace the midpoint sums by integrals:

```text
alpha_delta=integral_0^1 3(1-2u)^2 exp(i eta delta f(u)) du,
I_delta=integral_0^1 (1-2u) exp(i eta delta f(u)) du.
```

For either polynomial weight the absolute replacement error is o(delta).
To see this, cut at `u=epsilon delta`. The omitted integral and midpoint
weights cost `O(epsilon delta+1/q)`. Above the cut, (6) bounds the total
variation by `O(1+1/epsilon)`, hence the midpoint error by
`O(1/(q epsilon))`. Choose `epsilon=(q delta)^(-1/2)`.
The total is `O(sqrt(delta/q)+1/q)=o(delta)`. The exact normalization
of the first sum differs by the factor `q^2/(q^2-1)`; the identity
term in (4) is `3q^2/(q^2-1)` times the squared midpoint version of I.
Both normalization errors are harmless in this limit.

Since both polynomial weights have their stated zero-frequency integrals,
the bound `|exp(it)-1|<=min(2,|t|)` and f<=kappa/u imply
`|1-alpha_delta|, |I_delta|=O(delta[1+log(1/delta)])`.
For the leading real part set u=delta v:

```text
Re(1-alpha_delta)/delta
 -> 3 integral_0^infinity [1-cos(eta kappa/v)] dv
  = 3 pi kappa |eta|/2.                                  (12)
```

The limiting integrand follows from g(0+)=kappa. An integrable majorant
is a constant times `min(1,v^-2)`, so dominated convergence applies.
The last integral uses `int_0^infinity (1-cos t)/t^2 dt=pi/2`, obtained
by integration by parts and the exponentially regulated Dirichlet
integral. Finally
`1-|alpha|^2=2 Re(1-alpha)-|1-alpha|^2`; both this last square and
`3|I|^2` are o(delta). This proves (11), including eta=0 by (4).

Thus even though the local commuting central charge approaches q-4,
the two-frequency longitudinal vertex leaves the linear gauge-current
space at order delta in squared normalized norm. This is the order of
the previously proposed long-return absorption fraction, but it does
not prove an order-delta correction to that observable: its couplings,
propagators, state, Ward identities and stress insertions have not been
contracted. In particular, neither a central-charge substitution nor
equal-frequency conjugation supplies the missing interacting response.

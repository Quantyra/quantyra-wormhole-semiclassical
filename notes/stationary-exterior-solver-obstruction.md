# The full exterior requires a different solver from the isolated LLL system

S139/S074, 2026-10-15. Complete ordinary route correction; no checker or
numerical result is reported here. The retained free four-dimensional
Dirac action does not admit an unrestricted same-regularity contraction
obtained by applying a second-order Einstein inverse to its exact stress
response. This failure already occurs for static spherical perturbations
supported away from the horizon and the outer boundary. It is independent
of the still-open quantitative massive stress estimate.

The conclusion rejects that solver argument. It is not nonexistence of a
stationary semiclassical exterior, a macroscopic instability result or a
physical null. A higher-order boundary-value solve or a controlled reduced
branch remains to be constructed. The earlier isolated Polyakov-LLL
theorem and all frozen fixed-reference artifacts retain their scopes.

## Retained action and the exact scalar equation

Use the mostly-plus convention and the same free massless unit-charge
Dirac field, magnetic flux, common subtraction and finite-coefficient
choice as [the spherical anomaly action](spherical-anomaly-action.md).
Write b_an for its derivative-anomaly coefficient, to distinguish it from
the reference sphere radius. With zero additional finite curvature terms,

```
16pi^2 A4=c C4^2-a E4+b_an Box R4-(2/3)F4^2,
c=1/20, a=11/360, b_an=1/30.                        (1)
```

The coefficient b_an in this declared representative follows from the
local Dirac heat coefficient and its fermion sign. The retained derivation
uses [Vassilevich, (3.27), (4.28) and section 7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0306138v3).
It is a scheme choice, not a scheme-independent measurement of a finite
gravitational coupling.

Classical Maxwell stress is traceless. For the complete free-Dirac
semiclassical equation the bulk trace is consequently

```
-R4+4Lambda=8pi G A4.                              (2)
```

This is the joint four-dimensional trace, including the radius equation;
one must not replace it by an orbit trace alone. The independent
Weyl-invariant determinant functional has zero four-dimensional bulk
trace under compact variations. Its state dependence cannot cancel the
Box R4 term in (1). Neither can a boundary functional cancel a compactly
supported bulk principal term.

For a static spherical metric in areal gauge,

```
ds4^2=-exp(2alpha)F dU^2+dr^2/F+r^2 dOmega_2^2,

R4=-F''-3alpha'F'-2F(alpha''+alpha'^2)
   -4F'/r-4F alpha'/r+2(1-F)/r^2.                 (3)
```

On classical magnetic RN-AdS,

```
alpha0=0,
F0=1+r^2/L4^2-2m/r+Q/r^2,
R0=-12/L4^2,
Box0 f=r^-2 partial_r(r^2 F0 partial_r f)
       for a static scalar f.                     (4)
```

Thus R0 is constant even though the other curvature invariants vary.

## A localized, gauge-invariant spherical witness

Choose a closed radial interval I strictly inside the exterior, with
F0>=f_min>0, and a nonzero smooth real cutoff chi supported in its
interior. Consider smooth perturbations

```
delta F=0, delta alpha=a(r), supp(a) subset interior(I).
```

They preserve areal gauge, the outer data, the bolt metric and the
Euclidean HH period. Small multiples preserve Lorentzian signature and
positive F. Direct differentiation of (3) gives the exact identity

```
delta R[a]=-2F0 a''-(3F0'+4F0/r)a'.                (5)
```

Under an infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by xi, a scalar curvature
perturbation changes by Lie_xi R0. This vanishes because (4) is constant.
Therefore delta R in (5) is gauge invariant at this reference. A nonzero
witness is not a pure coordinate perturbation.

Since the variation of Box acting on the constant R0 is zero,

```
delta(Box R4)=Box0 delta R[a]
             =-2F0^2 a''''+an operator of order at most three on a.
                                                               (6)
```

The variations of C4^2 and E4 contain at most two derivatives of a.
For this particular perturbation the purely magnetic F4^2 is unchanged.
Equations (1), (5) and (6) hence give

```
8pi G delta A4[a]
 = (G/(60pi)) Box0 delta R[a]+L_2 a,               (7)
```

where L_2 is a fixed smooth differential operator of order at most two
on I. Its coefficients can depend on the reference. No estimate on the
separate massive determinant was used to obtain (7).

## No bounded second-order Einstein update on a fixed Sobolev scale

Here is the precise necessary condition on the proposed solver. Let a
smooth input metric perturbation h produce an output k by solving a
linearized classical Einstein-Maxwell equation with the exact quantum
stress variation as source. The trace of that classical operator has at
most two metric derivatives. At a classical RN solution it is exactly
-delta R[k]: the classical equation vanishes at the reference and the
Maxwell trace vanishes identically.

Taking this trace of the proposed update gives a necessary relation of
the form

```
-delta R[k]=8pi G delta A4[h]+B_0 h.               (8)
```

The algebraic term B_0 accounts for differentiating the metric used to
contract the fixed reference quantum stress: trace_g0(delta T) differs
from delta(trace_g T) by that algebraic contraction. It is smooth and
bounded on I. Allowing further bounded terms of differential order at
most two on the input or output does not change the argument below.

Equation (8) is only a necessary trace condition. We do not assume that
a gauge-fixed Einstein inverse exists, that an arbitrary trial source
satisfies every constraint, or that a chosen boundary problem has already
been solved. If a proposed full update exists, it must satisfy this
condition. If its constraint problem fails, that cannot rescue the
claimed contraction.

Fix an integer s>=4 and work with H^s radial norms on I. Smooth positive
metric and volume factors make the corresponding spherical tensor norms
equivalent on this compact interval. Let

```
a_n(r)=n^(-s) chi(r) cos(n r), n->infinity,          (9)
```

in a fixed radial length unit. The associated metric perturbations have
uniformly bounded H^s norm, bounded away from zero. Multiplying every
a_n by a common arbitrarily small constant puts the sequence in any
chosen neighborhood of the reference in that norm.

The fourth-order principal term in (7) is
-G F0^2 a_n''''/(30pi). Its H^(s-2) norm grows as a positive constant
times G n^2. The third-order remainder grows at most as n, and the
order-two terms in (7)-(8) stay bounded. These statements follow directly
by differentiating the cutoff oscillation; F0^2 chi is nonzero.
Meanwhile, for every smooth output k,

```
||delta R[k]||_(H^(s-2)(I)) <=C_I ||k||_(H^s(I)).  (10)
```

If the input-output derivative were bounded from H^s to the same H^s
space, (8)-(10) would bound its left side uniformly along (9), while
its right side diverges as n^2. This is a contradiction. More explicitly,
any outputs satisfying (8) obey

```
||k_n||_(H^s) >=c_I G n^2-C_I' n-C_I'',            (11)
```

with c_I>0 and fixed reference-dependent constants. Thus there is no
bounded linear response of this Einstein-preconditioned type on that
same space. A locally Lipschitz contraction is also excluded: along
every smooth direction its derivative would have to obey the uniform
Lipschitz bound, contradicting (11). The analogous cutoff construction
with n^(-k) proves the same loss on every fixed C^k scale with k>=4.
Increasing a finite regularity index does not remove a loss of two
derivatives.

For s>2 the pointwise curvature amplitude of these witnesses tends to
zero as n^(2-s), even while their wavelengths shrink. An explicit
low-momentum restriction can exclude this sequence; the theorem does
not exclude a contraction on such a restricted class. That restriction
would need its own covariant constraint prescription and truncation
error, rather than following from small background curvature alone.

Formally applying a scalar second-order trace inverse displays the same
principal multiplier,

```
delta alpha_out ~ +(G F0/(60pi)) p_r^2 delta alpha_in.
                                                               (12)
```

Equation (12) is an interpretation of the already established trace
contradiction, not an assumption that the scalar inverse solves the full
constraints. The proof does not require a transverse-traceless
polarization or a normalization of the nonlocal spin-two logarithm.

## The branch symbol and the finite-coefficient caveat

Linearizing the complete trace equation (2), rather than performing the
update, gives

```
(1+G Box0/(60pi)) delta R[a]+L_2 a=0.              (13)
```

Freezing coefficients locally and taking a static radial Fourier mode
produces the factor 1-G F0 p_r^2/(60pi). In proper radial momentum the
principal factor has a zero at p_proper^2=60pi/G. This identifies the
additional short-distance branch of the higher-order equation. It is
only a local frozen-coefficient symbol: no globally admissible mode,
global instability, or boundary-value eigenfunction has been proved.
The fixed background source can be very small while this high-frequency
derivative remains unbounded.

An added Lorentzian term c_R integral sqrt(-g)R4^2 changes the total
coefficient to

```
b_total=1/30-192pi^2 c_R,
G/(60pi) -> G b_total/(2pi).                       (14)
```

If b_total is zero this particular scalar obstruction disappears. The
selected zero-extra-coefficient model has c_R=0 and b_total=1/30, so
that exception is not available by declaration. Moving the same local
term to the geometric side and inverting its fourth-order principal
operator would be a different preconditioner, not a second-order
Einstein contraction. This scalar proof makes no claim about every
possible scheme or the remaining traceless response when b_total=0.

## Primary-source comparison and the required route correction

[Anderson--Molina-Paris--Mottola, sections II-III](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0209075)
derive the renormalized higher-derivative gravitational equations and
the stress-response kernel. Their analysis distinguishes the low-energy
semiclassical regime from additional short-distance solutions. Their
worked stability example is a scalar field in Minkowski space; it is
not a stationary RN/HH existence theorem. Equations (5)-(11) above give
the specific spherical obstruction for the coefficient retained here.

[Simon, Phys. Rev. D 43, 3308](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.43.3308)
explains the need to exclude solutions outside a controlled perturbative
branch. [Flanagan--Wald, section IV.D, pp24-27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9602052)
give a reduction-of-order construction in their perturbative setting.
They also explicitly distinguish removal of higher time derivatives
from removal of higher spatial derivatives. Their prescription is not
an automatic static radial reduction on a general black-hole geometry.

The selected correction is to reject an unqualified same-regularity
Einstein contraction for the full determinant. Two possible routes
remain explicit:

- A full higher-order or pseudodifferential boundary-value method must
  specify its regularity spaces, constraints, physical boundary data and
  inverse, and control the extra branches. A small source amplitude
  alone supplies none of those properties.
- A controlled reduced branch must state the expansion and residual
  order, derive a covariant constraint-compatible spatial reduction,
  and specify how the additional solutions are excluded. It must retain
  the corrected product center, outer vacuum prescription and prepared
  temperature; expansion about the old unchanged horizon is not restored
  by choosing this route. Stress, radius, boundary and state terms remain
  part of the same accounting.

Neither route is selected as accomplished by this note. In particular,
the existing heat coefficients and the proposed frozen-mass Born series
do not yet give the quantified global renormalized metric response:
the low-Born terms still need their common angular UV subtraction and
an explicit differentiated remainder. No tentative loop constant is
being promoted into a physical lapse bound. Those source estimates are
still valuable for a specified solver, but do not eliminate (11).
